How the severity zones work

Predictive maintenance replaces fixed service calendars with measurement: a machine is serviced when its condition says so, before it fails.

The measured quantity is RMS velocity, written vRMS in millimetres per second, taken on the bearing housing in three directions. The scale below uses the limits for Group 2 machines on a rigid foundation.

Severity zones, from best to worst

  1. Newly commissioned condition: up to 1.4 mm/s.
  2. Acceptable for unrestricted long-term operation: 1.4 to 2.8 mm/s.
  3. Unsatisfactory for continuous operation, plan remedial work: 2.8 to 4.5 mm/s.
  4. Likely to cause damage: above 4.5 mm/s. Report immediately. The machine is stopped and locked out under the site procedure before inspection.
Why does the foundation type matter?

Machines on a flexible foundation, such as the compressor on its spring skid, are allowed higher readings. The same 3.8 mm/s is Zone B for CP-01 but would be Zone C on a rigid base.

Why flag a change inside Zone B?

A fast rise is a warning even while the level is still acceptable. The page flags any change larger than a quarter of the B/C limit since the previous reading.

Why is no flexibly mounted fan in the fleet?

The standard's criteria generally apply only to fans above 300 kW or fans that are not flexibly supported, so the fan here sits on a rigid base.

Field procedure

Walk the route with the handheld meter, check each machine, and follow the steps below when a reading is out of range.

Inspection checklist

  • Bearing housing is warm, not hot, to the back of the hand.
  • Mounting bolts are tight and the base shows no cracks.
  • Coupling guard is in place and secure.
  • Lubrication points show fresh grease and no leakage.
  • Sensor cable is intact and clipped away from moving parts.

When a reading falls in Zone C or D

  1. Repeat the measurement at the same point and direction to rule out a sensor error.
  2. Compare the value with the machine's trend above.
  3. Record the reading and your observations in the form below.
  4. Notify the maintenance supervisor by email.
  5. For Zone D, wait for the supervisor's lockout before anyone opens a guard.

Inspection interval for Zone C machines: monthly weekly, revised this quarter after two bearing failures.
